A World Begun — soundtrack brief

Loading / intro music and in-game map music · commissioning brief, August 2026

The game: a turn-based 4X strategy game in the tradition of the 1991 original — you lead a civilization from 4000 BC to the space age. It runs in a browser and on Roblox.

The one fact that shapes everything below: a session lasts one to six hours, mostly on a single screen, with the player thinking. Not a shooter where music is heard in ninety-second bursts.

The game already has its sound effects — roughly thirty short cues for combat, building, discoveries and so on, generated in the browser. Those stay as they are and are not part of this commission. What we are commissioning is the music: the title theme and the music that plays over the map.

1. The three rules that matter more than any single track

  1. It has to survive hour three. A theme that delights on loop 2 can be unbearable on loop 40. Slow harmonic movement, long phrases, melody suggested rather than hammered. If a hook is catchy enough to hum after one listen, it is probably too strong for the map layer.
  2. It plays under thinking, not over action. The player is reading numbers, comparing tiles, weighing a war. The music must sit below attention and stay there — sparse, unhurried, with air in it.
  3. Silence is a legitimate instrument. We would rather have six excellent minutes that breathe than twenty minutes of continuous sound. Tracks may rest; the engine can leave gaps between loops.
The anti-goal, stated plainly because it is the usual failure: no epic orchestral bombast on the map layer. Save intensity for the intro and the endings, where it is earned and heard once.

2. Track list

Lengths are the composed length before looping. Priority 1 is what we would commission first — the game is complete and better with only that set.

loading-ageA real wait: centuries being simulated before play starts0:30–1:00yes2

intro-title is the one everybody hears. It is the first impression, and it plays while the player chooses a civilization, a map and a difficulty — so it may be heard for twenty seconds or four minutes. Theme: the beginning of things. Curiosity and open horizon rather than triumph; something that could be a folk melody from anywhere on Earth rather than one specific tradition. Restrained percussion, or none. It should feel like before history, not like a war about to start. This is the one track allowed a real, memorable melody — it is heard at the start of a session, not for three hours.

loading-age covers a genuine wait while the world is fast-forwarded through centuries. Theme: time passing and accumulating — a repeating figure that gains a layer every few bars, then resets. Functional music that makes a wait feel like a process rather than a hang.

B. The map layer, by era — the heart of the commission

The game moves through five ages, and the music changes with them. This is the main musical arc of a session: the same world, four times older.

TrackEraLengthLoopsPriority
map-ancient4000 BC → classical3:00–5:00yes1
map-renaissanceClassical → industrial3:00–5:00yes1
map-industrialIndustrial age3:00–5:00yes1
map-modernModern age3:00–5:00yes2
map-spaceSpace age (endgame)2:00–4:00yes2

The arc, given as instrumentation rather than adjectives so it is actionable:

Ancient — few voices, wide space, no harmony in a hurry. Bone flute, frame drum, low drone, a single human voice used as texture. The world is mostly unknown; it should feel like not knowing what is over the hill.
Renaissance — more voices, real counterpoint, plucked and bowed strings. Confidence and craft: the world is being mapped and argued about. Still no drums of war.
Industrial — rhythm arrives, and it is mechanical rather than martial. Repetition, pulse, iron. This is where material from the ancient track can return transformed — the strongest single moment available to us, if it appeals to you.
Modern — thinner and colder rather than bigger. Sustained tones, electric texture, less human breath. The world is fully known and mostly spoken for.
Space — genuinely quiet, high and open, almost weightless. The endgame runs long and tense, so this must be the calmest track of the set, not the loudest.
Wanted, not required — and the highest-value idea in this brief: a small motif of three to five notes that can survive all five treatments. If it does, the era changes land as a story rather than a playlist.

C. Water-world colour (optional, priority 3)

The game has nine map shapes; most need nothing. Two are distinctive enough to feel a variant: worlds that are mostly ocean, and worlds built around one central sea. If it appeals, a single map-ancient-water variant — same material, different instrumentation (breath, resonance, less earth). Please do not write five more full tracks for this; it is seasoning.

The endings are heard once per game, at the moment a session of several hours resolves. They are the only place here where full intensity is right — and end-defeat should be genuinely sad rather than ominous. The player just lost several hours; respect it.

3. How much music, honestly

Priority 1 alone is four tracks, roughly 13–18 minutes — a complete, shippable soundtrack. Priority 2 adds another 8–12 minutes. Priority 3 is optional colour.

Please do not deliver 45 minutes. A smaller set that is genuinely good under repetition beats a larger set that is merely present.

4. Mix and loudness

  • −20 to −18 LUFS integrated for map tracks — deliberately quiet. They sit under sound effects and under a person concentrating. Intro and endings may sit about 3 dB hotter.
  • Leave 1–4 kHz uncluttered where you can. Our effect cues live there, and a player must hear a combat result over the music without us ducking it.
  • True peak ≤ −1 dBTP, no brickwall limiting, no aggressive multiband compression. Dynamic range is a feature here.
  • Stereo, but check in mono — many players are on phone speakers.

5. Delivery

  • Masters: WAV, 48 kHz, 24-bit, one file per track, named exactly by the track id above — map-ancient.wav.
  • Loops: ideally seamless (the last bar flows into the first). If not, send loop-start and loop-end sample positions in a text file.
  • Stems are optional but valuable: if the era tracks share material, stems let us cross-fade between eras instead of cutting.
  • We handle web encoding and the Roblox upload — please don't pre-compress.
  • A rough sketch (phone recording, piano) of intro-title and map-ancient before full production is very welcome. Those two set the palette for everything else.

6. How the game uses it

  • One track at a time. The era track changes when the player's technology crosses an age boundary, cross-faded over about four seconds — never cut.
  • Music has its own on/off control and volume, separate from sound effects. A player can turn music off entirely and every gameplay cue still works.
  • Loops can be separated by a short configurable silence, so a four-minute track never feels like a four-minute treadmill.
  • Endings and stings duck the map music rather than stopping it.

7. How we will judge it

  • The three-hour test. We loop each map track for an hour while playing. If we reach for the volume slider, it fails — however beautiful it is on first listen.
  • The phone test. Audible and pleasant in mono on a phone speaker.
  • The cue test. A combat result and a discovery chime stay clearly audible over the music, with no ducking.
  • The seam test. Loop it ten times and listen for the join.
  • The arc test. Played back to back, ancient → renaissance → industrial should feel like one world ageing, not three unrelated pieces.

8. Rights — please confirm before starting

This is an original game, freely available, with open-source code. It is inspired by a 1991 title but uses none of its assets. The music must be original and free of sample-library licence terms that would prevent distribution in a free game and inside a Roblox experience. Please confirm the intended rights grant in writing before production — far easier now than afterwards.
